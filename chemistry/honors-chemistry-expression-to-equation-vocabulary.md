# The Chemistry Reflex: Expression → Equation Flashcard Deck

> **If you can calculate it, you have understood it.**
>
> This deck trains one reflex: **see a phrase → write the equation.** No hesitation. No "which formula?" No "do I use moles or molarity?" The word itself is the command. Memorize these mappings the way you memorized vocabulary for the SAT. When you sit down for the exam, the equations will already be on the page before you finish reading the problem.

---

## How to Use This Deck

1. **Cover the right column.** Read the trigger phrase. Say the equation aloud. Uncover. Check.
2. **Drill by domain.** Master Stoichiometry before touching Equilibrium. Each domain is self-contained.
3. **Daily minimum:** 25 cards. Rotate domains. The deck has 213 cards — you can cycle through it in about 8–9 days.
4. **Exam simulation:** When doing practice problems, use Ctrl+F on this document. The goal is to need Ctrl+F less and less.

**Signal system:**
- ⚡ = High-frequency exam trigger. Appears in 50%+ of problems.
- 🔥 = Distinguisher. Separates A students from B students.
- 🧠 = Memorization item. A constant or value you just need to know.

## 1. Stoichiometry — "How Much Stuff"

> **Core reflex:** "grams," "moles," "molar mass," "limiting," "yield" → convert to moles → use mole ratio → convert back.

### 1.1 Mole Conversions

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 1 ⚡ | "moles", "number of moles $n$" | $n = \frac{m}{M}$ | $m$ = mass (g), $M$ = molar mass (g/mol). The mole bridge between mass and particles. |
| 2 ⚡ | "molar mass $M$" | $M = \frac{m}{n}$ (g/mol) | Sum of atomic masses from periodic table. |
| 3 ⚡ | "grams to moles", "convert mass to moles" | $n = \frac{\text{mass (g)}}{M\;\mathrm{(g/mol)}}$ | Always convert to moles first — never do stoichiometry in grams. |
| 4 ⚡ | "moles to particles", "number of molecules/atoms" | $N = n \times N_A$ | $N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}\;\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$ (Avogadro's number). |
| 5 🧠 | "Avogadro's number $N_A$" | $N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}\;\mathrm{mol^{-1}}$ | Number of particles in one mole. |
| 6 | "moles to volume" (gas at STP) | $V = n \times 22.4\;\mathrm{L/mol}$ | Only at STP: $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $1\;\mathrm{atm}$. For other conditions, use Ideal Gas Law. |
| 7 | "STP" | $T = 273\;\mathrm{K}$, $P = 1\;\mathrm{atm}$, $V_m = 22.4\;\mathrm{L/mol}$ | Standard Temperature and Pressure. |

### 1.2 Chemical Equations & Mole Ratios

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 8 ⚡ | "balanced equation", "stoichiometric coefficients" | $a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \to c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}$ | Coefficients $a,b,c,d$ are the mole ratios. |
| 9 ⚡ | "mole ratio", "stoichiometric ratio" | $\frac{n_{\mathrm{A}}}{a} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{B}}}{b} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{C}}}{c}$ | Use coefficients from balanced equation. Bridge between any two substances. |
| 10 ⚡ | "grams of A → grams of B" | $\text{g A} \xrightarrow{\div M_A} n_{\mathrm{A}} \xrightarrow{\text{ratio}} n_{\mathrm{B}} \xrightarrow{\times M_B} \text{g B}$ | **The three-step path.** Never skip moles — different substances have different molar masses, so grams do NOT follow the mole ratio. Always: mass → moles → moles → mass. |
| 11 ⚡ | "limiting reagent", "limiting reactant" | Calculate $n$ for each reactant; divide by its coefficient. **Smallest = limiting.** | The reactant that runs out first. Determines theoretical yield. |
| 12 🔥 | "theoretical yield" | Mass of product calculated from limiting reagent via mole ratio. | Maximum possible product. Always based on limiting reagent. |
| 13 🔥 | "percent yield", "% yield" | $\%\;\text{yield} = \frac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100\%$ | Always ≤ 100% unless experimental error. |
| 14 | "excess reagent" | The reactant NOT limiting. Some remains unreacted. | Calculate leftover: initial mol − mol consumed (from limiting reagent × mole ratio). |

### 1.3 Empirical & Molecular Formulas

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 15 | "empirical formula" | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. | From mass %: assume $100\;\mathrm{g}$ → mass → moles → divide by smallest → whole numbers. |
| 16 🔥 | "molecular formula" | $(EF)_n$ where $n = \frac{M_{\text{molecular}}}{M_{\text{empirical}}}$ | Molecular formula = empirical formula × integer $n$. |
| 17 | "percent composition", "mass percent" | $\%\;\text{element} = \frac{\text{mass of element in 1 mol}}{\text{molar mass of compound}} \times 100\%$ | |
| 18 | "combustion analysis" | C → CO₂, H → H₂O. From masses of CO₂ and H₂O, find mass of C and H. | Oxygen % by difference: $100\% - \%C - \%H$. |

## 2. Gases — "Things That Expand to Fill"

> **Core reflex:** "Gas," "pressure," "temperature" → $PV = nRT$ or its children.

### 2.1 The Ideal Gas Law

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 19 ⚡ | "ideal gas", "$PV = nRT$" | $PV = nRT$ | $R = 0.08206\;\mathrm{L\;atm/(mol\;K)} = 8.314\;\mathrm{J/(mol\;K)}$. Pick $R$ based on units. |
| 20 🧠 | "gas constant $R$" | $R = 0.08206\;\mathrm{L\;atm\;mol^{-1}\;K^{-1}} = 8.314\;\mathrm{J\;mol^{-1}\;K^{-1}}$ | Two values — match the pressure unit. |
| 21 ⚡ | "sealed container", "fixed $n$" | $\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$ | Combined Gas Law. Use when $n$ is constant. |
| 22 | "Boyle's Law" ($T$ constant) | $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ | $P \propto 1/V$. Pressure and volume inversely proportional. |
| 23 | "Charles's Law" ($P$ constant) | $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ | $V \propto T$. Temperature MUST be in Kelvin. |
| 24 | "Gay-Lussac's Law" ($V$ constant) | $\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$ | $P \propto T$. Pressure increases with temperature. |
| 25 | "Avogadro's Law" ($T$, $P$ constant) | $\frac{V_1}{n_1} = \frac{V_2}{n_2}$ | Equal volumes contain equal moles at same $T$ and $P$. |

### 2.2 Gas Mixtures & Real Gases

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 26 ⚡ | "partial pressure", "Dalton's Law" | $P_{\text{total}} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \cdots$ | Each gas behaves independently. |
| 27 🔥 | "mole fraction $X_A$" | $X_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{\text{total}}}$, $P_A = X_A P_{\text{total}}$ | Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure. |
| 28 | "collecting gas over water" | $P_{\text{gas}} = P_{\text{total}} - P_{\text{H₂O}}$ | Subtract water vapor pressure. |
| 29 🔥 | "Graham's Law of Effusion" | $\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_1}}$ | Lighter gas effuses faster. Rate ∝ $1/\sqrt{M}$. |
| 30 🔥 | "root-mean-square speed $v_{\text{rms}}$" | $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$ | ⚠️ $M$ MUST be in **kg/mol** (divide g/mol by 1000). Most common mistake: using g/mol and getting speeds 31.6× too small. Lighter molecules move faster. |
| 31 | "kinetic energy of gas" | Per mole: $\bar{K}_{\text{mol}} = \frac{3}{2}RT$. Per molecule: $\bar{K} = \frac{3}{2}k_B T$. | Two forms. Use $R = 8.314$ when working per mole; use $k_B = 1.38\times10^{-23}\;\mathrm{J/K}$ per molecule. Temperature IS average kinetic energy, up to this constant factor. |
| 32 | "van der Waals equation" | $(P + a\frac{n^2}{V^2})(V - nb) = nRT$ | $a$ = attraction, $b$ = volume of molecules. Real gases. |

## 3. Thermochemistry — "Heat in Chemical Reactions"

> **Core reflex:** "Heat," "enthalpy," "$\Delta H$" → $q = mc\Delta T$ or Hess's Law.

### 3.1 Heat and Calorimetry

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 33 ⚡ | "heat", "thermal energy $q$" (with $\Delta T$) | $q = mc\Delta T$ | $c$ = specific heat capacity ($\mathrm{J\;g^{-1}\;K^{-1}}$). |
| 34 🧠 | "specific heat of water $c_w$" | $c_w = 4.184\;\mathrm{J\;g^{-1}\;K^{-1}} = 4.184\;\mathrm{J\;g^{-1}\;{}^\circ C^{-1}}$ | Memorize this. Most common value in calorimetry. |
| 35 | "heat capacity $C$" | $C = mc$ (extensive); $q = C\Delta T$ | $C$ = heat needed to raise temperature of entire object by 1 K. |
| 36 ⚡ | "calorimetry", "coffee-cup calorimeter" | $q_{\text{system}} + q_{\text{surroundings}} = 0$ | Hot loses = cold gains. Constant pressure. |
| 37 🔥 | "bomb calorimeter" | $q_{\text{rxn}} = -C_{\text{cal}}\Delta T$ | Constant volume. $C_{\text{cal}}$ = heat capacity of calorimeter. |
| 38 | "molar heat capacity $C_m$" | $q = n C_m \Delta T$ | Per mole instead of per gram. |

### 3.2 Enthalpy

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 39 ⚡ | "enthalpy change $\Delta H$" | $\Delta H = H_{\text{products}} - H_{\text{reactants}}$ | Negative = exothermic. Positive = endothermic. |
| 40 ⚡ | "exothermic" | $\Delta H < 0$, heat released | Products have lower enthalpy. Reaction vessel feels hot. |
| 41 ⚡ | "endothermic" | $\Delta H > 0$, heat absorbed | Products have higher enthalpy. Reaction vessel feels cold. |
| 42 ⚡ | "Hess's Law" | $\Delta H_{\text{rxn}} = \sum \Delta H_{\text{steps}}$ | Enthalpy is a state function — path independent. |
| 43 🔥 | "standard enthalpy of formation $\Delta H_f^\circ$" | $\Delta H_{\text{rxn}}^\circ = \sum n\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum n\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{reactants})$ | $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of elements in standard state = 0. |
| 44 🔥 | "bond energy", "bond enthalpy" | $\Delta H_{\text{rxn}} \approx \sum \text{BE}(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum \text{BE}(\text{bonds formed})$ | Breaking bonds = endothermic (+). Forming bonds = exothermic (−). |
| 45 | "standard state" | $298\;\mathrm{K}$, $1\;\mathrm{atm}$ (or $1\;\mathrm{bar}$), $1\;\mathrm{M}$ for solutions | Superscript ° means standard state. |

### 3.3 Entropy and Gibbs Free Energy

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 46 ⚡ | "entropy $S$" | $\Delta S_{\text{universe}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} > 0$ | Measure of disorder. Universe entropy always increases. |
| 47 🔥 | "standard entropy $\Delta S^\circ$" | $\Delta S^\circ = \sum nS^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum nS^\circ(\text{reactants})$ | Similar form to $\Delta H^\circ$. |
| 48 ⚡ | "Gibbs free energy $\Delta G$" | $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$ | The ultimate spontaneity criterion. ⚠️ $T$ MUST be in Kelvin. ⚠️ $\Delta S$ is usually given in J/(mol·K) but $\Delta H$ in kJ/mol — **convert one to match the other** before subtracting. |
| 49 ⚡ | "spontaneous" | $\Delta G < 0$ | Negative $\Delta G$ = reaction proceeds forward. |
| 50 🔥 | "$\Delta G$ at non-standard conditions" | $\Delta G = \Delta G^\circ + RT\ln Q$ | $Q$ = reaction quotient. |
| 51 🔥 | "$\Delta G^\circ$ and equilibrium" | $\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K$ | At equilibrium, $\Delta G = 0$. Bridge between thermo and equilibrium. |
| 52 | "$\Delta G^\circ$ from $\Delta H^\circ$ and $\Delta S^\circ$" | $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$ | All standard state values. |

## 4. Atomic Structure & Periodicity

> **Core reflex:** "Electron configuration," "quantum number," "periodic trend" → Aufbau, $n$, $\ell$, $m_\ell$, $m_s$.

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 53 🧠 | "quantum numbers" | $n$ (principal), $\ell$ (azimuthal: 0 to $n-1$), $m_\ell$ (magnetic: $-\ell$ to $+\ell$), $m_s$ (spin: $\pm\frac{1}{2}$) | Four quantum numbers uniquely identify every electron. |
| 54 ⚡ | "electron configuration" | Aufbau: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^2 3d^{10}\ldots$ | Fill lowest energy first. Exceptions: Cr $[\mathrm{Ar}]4s^1 3d^5$, Cu $[\mathrm{Ar}]4s^1 3d^{10}$. |
| 55 🔥 | "Hund's Rule" | Maximize unpaired electrons with parallel spins in degenerate orbitals. | "Empty bus seat rule." |
| 56 | "Pauli Exclusion Principle" | No two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers. | Each orbital holds max 2 electrons (opposite spins). |
| 57 🧠 | "max electrons in shell $n$" | $2n^2$ | $n=1$: 2, $n=2$: 8, $n=3$: 18, $n=4$: 32. |
| 58 🧠 | "orbitals in subshell" | s: 1, p: 3, d: 5, f: 7 | Each orbital holds 2 electrons. |
| 59 🔥 | "atomic radius trend" | Decreases → across period. Increases ↓ down group. | Effective nuclear charge $Z_{\text{eff}}$ increases across period. |
| 60 🔥 | "ionization energy trend" | Increases → across period. Decreases ↓ down group. | Opposite of atomic radius. |
| 61 🔥 | "electronegativity trend" | Increases → across period. Decreases ↓ down group. | F is most electronegative (4.0 on Pauling scale). |
| 62 | "electron affinity" | Energy released when gaining an electron. | Halogens have most negative (most favorable) EA. |
| 63 | "isoelectronic" | Same electron configuration. | e.g., $\mathrm{F^-}$, $\mathrm{Ne}$, $\mathrm{Na^+}$, $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ all $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6$. |

## 5. Bonding & Molecular Structure

> **Core reflex:** "Lewis structure," "VSEPR," "hybridization" → count valence electrons → arrange → predict shape.

### 5.1 Lewis Structures & Formal Charge

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 64 ⚡ | "Lewis structure" | Count valence electrons. Form bonds (2 e⁻ each). Complete octets. | H gets 2 e⁻. B and Be can be electron-deficient. Period 3+ can expand octet. |
| 65 🔥 | "formal charge" | $\mathrm{FC} = \text{valence } e^- - (\text{nonbonding } e^- + \frac{1}{2}\text{bonding } e^-)$ | Sum of all formal charges = charge on the species (ion). Best Lewis structure: formal charges closest to **zero** on every atom. Negative FC on more electronegative atoms is acceptable; positive FC on electronegative atoms is not. |
| 66 🔥 | "resonance" | Multiple valid Lewis structures. Actual structure is hybrid. | Delocalized electrons. Bond order = bonds/connections. |
| 67 | "bond order" | $\mathrm{BO} = \frac{N_{\text{bonding}} - N_{\text{antibonding}}}{2}$ (MO theory). For Lewis structures: BO = number of shared pairs between two atoms. | Higher bond order = stronger bond, shorter bond length. Triple > Double > Single. |

### 5.2 VSEPR & Hybridization

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 68 ⚡ | "VSEPR", "molecular geometry" | Count electron domains (bonds + lone pairs) around central atom. | 2: linear, 3: trigonal planar, 4: tetrahedral, 5: trigonal bipyramidal, 6: octahedral. |
| 69 🔥 | "electron domain geometry" vs "molecular geometry" | Lone pairs occupy space but are NOT part of molecular shape. | NH₃: 4 domains (tetrahedral e⁻ geometry), 3 bonds + 1 LP → trigonal pyramidal molecular shape. |
| 70 🔥 | "hybridization $sp$, $sp^2$, $sp^3$" | # hybrid orbitals = # electron domains. | 2 domains = $sp$, 3 = $sp^2$, 4 = $sp^3$, 5 = $sp^3d$, 6 = $sp^3d^2$. |
| 71 | "bond angles" | $sp$: 180°, $sp^2$: 120°, $sp^3$: 109.5° | Lone pairs compress bond angles ($< 109.5°$ for NH₃, H₂O). |

### 5.3 Bond Properties

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 72 🔥 | "dipole moment $\mu$" | $\mu = q \times d$ | Polar bonds + asymmetric geometry → molecular dipole. |
| 73 | "polar molecule" | Has net dipole moment. Asymmetric + polar bonds. | CO₂ has polar bonds but is NONPOLAR (linear, dipoles cancel). |
| 74 | "lattice energy" | Energy released when gaseous ions form solid ionic compound. | Coulomb's Law: $E \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}$. Larger charges, smaller ions → larger lattice energy. |
| 75 | "bond length vs bond energy" | Triple > Double > Single (bond energy). Single > Double > Triple (bond length). | More bonds = stronger and shorter. |

## 6. Chemical Kinetics — "How Fast"

> **Core reflex:** "Rate," "order," "half-life," "activation energy" → rate law → integrated rate law → Arrhenius.

### 6.1 Rate Laws

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 76 ⚡ | "rate law" | $\text{Rate} = k[\mathrm{A}]^m[\mathrm{B}]^n$ | $m$, $n$ = reaction orders. Determined EXPERIMENTALLY, not from coefficients. |
| 77 ⚡ | "rate constant $k$" | Units depend on overall order: 0th: M/s, 1st: 1/s, 2nd: 1/(M·s). | $k$ increases with temperature (Arrhenius). |
| 78 🔥 | "determine reaction order" (method of initial rates) | $\frac{\text{Rate}_2}{\text{Rate}_1} = \left(\frac{[\mathrm{A}]_2}{[\mathrm{A}]_1}\right)^m$ | Compare two experiments. Works ONLY when **all other reactant concentrations are held constant** between those two trials. Find the order for each reactant one at a time; the overall order is their sum (Card 79). |
| 79 | "overall reaction order" | Sum of individual orders: $m + n + \cdots$ | |

### 6.2 Integrated Rate Laws

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 80 ⚡ | "zero-order reaction" | $[\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{A}]_0 - kt$ | Linear plot: $[\mathrm{A}]$ vs $t$. $t_{1/2} = [\mathrm{A}]_0/(2k)$. |
| 81 ⚡ | "first-order reaction" | $\ln[\mathrm{A}] = \ln[\mathrm{A}]_0 - kt$ | Linear plot: $\ln[\mathrm{A}]$ vs $t$. $t_{1/2} = \ln 2/k = 0.693/k$. |
| 82 ⚡ | "second-order reaction" | $\frac{1}{[\mathrm{A}]} = \frac{1}{[\mathrm{A}]_0} + kt$ | Linear plot: $1/[\mathrm{A}]$ vs $t$. $t_{1/2} = 1/(k[\mathrm{A}]_0)$. |
| 83 🔥 | "half-life $t_{1/2}$" | 0th: $\frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{2k}$, 1st: $\frac{0.693}{k}$, 2nd: $\frac{1}{k[\mathrm{A}]_0}$ | Only 1st-order half-life is independent of concentration. |

### 6.3 Temperature Dependence & Mechanisms

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 84 ⚡ | "Arrhenius equation" | $k = A e^{-E_a/(RT)}$ | $A$ = frequency factor, $E_a$ = activation energy (J/mol). |
| 85 🔥 | "two-point Arrhenius" | $\ln\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right)$ | Find $E_a$ from $k$ at two temperatures. |
| 86 🔥 | "activation energy $E_a$" | Minimum energy for reaction. From Arrhenius plot slope = $-E_a/R$. | Catalysts lower $E_a$. |
| 87 | "rate-determining step" | Slowest step in mechanism. Rate law from this step's molecularity. | Rate law matches the slow step's stoichiometry. |
| 88 | "catalyst" | Lowers $E_a$, provides alternate pathway. NOT consumed. | Does NOT affect $\Delta H$, $\Delta G$, or equilibrium constant $K$. |

## 7. Chemical Equilibrium — "When Reactions Go Both Ways"

> **Core reflex:** "Equilibrium," "$K_c$," "$K_p$," "Le Chatelier" → equilibrium expression → ICE table → solve.

### 7.1 Equilibrium Constants

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 89 ⚡ | "equilibrium constant $K_c$" | $K_c = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c[\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a[\mathrm{B}]^b}$ | Products over reactants. Each raised to its coefficient. |
| 90 🔥 | "$K_p$ from $K_c$" | $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$ | $\Delta n =$ (moles of gaseous products) − (moles of gaseous reactants). **Only count gases** — solids and liquids do NOT contribute to $\Delta n$. If $\Delta n = 0$, then $K_p = K_c$. |
| 91 🔥 | "reaction quotient $Q$" | $Q = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c[\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a[\mathrm{B}]^b}$ (same form as $K$, but at any point) | $Q < K$: forward. $Q = K$: equilibrium. $Q > K$: reverse. |
| 92 | "large $K$" vs "small $K$" | $K \gg 1$: products favored. $K \ll 1$: reactants favored. | $K \approx 1$: significant amounts of both. |

### 7.2 ICE Tables

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 93 ⚡ | "ICE table" | **I**nitial → **C**hange → **E**quilibrium | Row 1: initial concentrations. Row 2: change ($-ax$, $-bx$, $+cx$, $+dx$). Row 3: sum. |
| 94 🔥 | "small $K$ approximation" | If $K$ is small ($K < 10^{-4}$ is a rough guide), assume $x \ll [\text{initial}]$ and drop $x$ from subtraction terms: $[\mathrm{HA}]_0 - x \approx [\mathrm{HA}]_0$. | ⚠️ **Always verify afterward:** calculate $x/[\text{initial}]_0 \times 100\%$. If it exceeds $5\%$, the approximation is invalid — you must solve the full quadratic equation. |
| 95 | "equilibrium concentration" | From ICE table: $[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = [\mathrm{A}]_0 - ax$ | $x$ from solving $K$ expression. |

### 7.3 Le Chatelier's Principle

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 96 ⚡ | "Le Chatelier's Principle" | System shifts to relieve applied stress. | Stress: concentration, pressure/volume, temperature. |
| 97 | "add reactant/product" | Shifts AWAY from added substance. | Add A → forward shift (consumes A). Add C → reverse shift. |
| 98 🔥 | "change volume/pressure" (gases) | Increase $P$ (decrease $V$) → shift toward FEWER gas moles. | Only affects gaseous equilibria with $\Delta n \neq 0$. |
| 99 🔥 | "change temperature" | Exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$): increase $T$ → shifts LEFT. Endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$): increase $T$ → shifts RIGHT. | Only temperature changes $K$. |
| 100 | "catalyst effect on equilibrium" | Does NOT shift equilibrium. Speeds up both forward and reverse equally. | $K$ unchanged. |

## 8. Acids & Bases — "Proton Transfer"

> **Core reflex:** "pH," "acid," "base," "buffer," "titration" → $K_a$, $K_b$, $K_w$, Henderson-Hasselbalch.

### 8.1 pH and Dissociation Constants

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 101 ⚡ | "pH" | $\mathrm{pH} = -\log[\mathrm{H}^+]$ | $[\mathrm{H}^+] = 10^{-\mathrm{pH}}$. |
| 102 ⚡ | "pOH" | $\mathrm{pOH} = -\log[\mathrm{OH}^-]$ | $\mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = 14.00$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$. |
| 103 🧠 | "ion product of water $K_w$" | $K_w = [\mathrm{H}^+][\mathrm{OH}^-] = 1.0\times10^{-14}$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ | $K_w$ increases with temperature. |
| 104 ⚡ | "acid dissociation constant $K_a$" | $K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H}^+][\mathrm{A}^-]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$ | Larger $K_a$ = stronger acid. $\mathrm{p}K_a = -\log K_a$. |
| 105 ⚡ | "base dissociation constant $K_b$" | $K_b = \frac{[\mathrm{BH}^+][\mathrm{OH}^-]}{[\mathrm{B}]}$ | $K_a \times K_b = K_w$ for conjugate acid-base pair. |
| 106 🔥 | "percent ionization" | $\%\;\text{ionization} = \frac{[\mathrm{H}^+]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{HA}]_0} \times 100\%$ | Weak acids: % ionization increases with dilution. |

### 8.2 Strong vs Weak Acids & Bases

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 107 🧠 | "strong acids" (memorize) | HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ (first H⁺), HClO₄, HClO₃ | Completely dissociate. $[\mathrm{H}^+] = [\mathrm{HA}]_0$. |
| 108 🧠 | "strong bases" (memorize) | Group 1 hydroxides: LiOH, NaOH, KOH, RbOH, CsOH. Also Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂. | Completely dissociate. |
| 109 🔥 | "weak acid pH" | $[\mathrm{H}^+] = \sqrt{K_a[\mathrm{HA}]_0}$ | Derived from ICE table with the small-$K_a$ approximation. Valid ONLY when (a) $K_a$ is small relative to $[\mathrm{HA}]_0$, AND (b) $[\mathrm{HA}]_0$ is not extremely dilute (water's autoionization is negligible). Always verify the 5% rule (Card 94). |
| 110 🔥 | "weak base pOH" | $[\mathrm{OH}^-] = \sqrt{K_b[\mathrm{B}]_0}$ (if % ionization < 5%) | Then $\mathrm{pH} = 14 - \mathrm{pOH}$. |

### 8.3 Buffers & Titrations

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 111 ⚡ | "buffer", "buffer solution" | Weak acid + its conjugate base (or weak base + conjugate acid). | Resists pH change. |
| 112 ⚡ | "Henderson-Hasselbalch equation" | $\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\mathrm{A}^-]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$ | For buffers. $[\mathrm{A}^-]$ and $[\mathrm{HA}]$ are **equilibrium** concentrations, but for buffers they approximately equal the initial (prepared) concentrations. Valid when the ratio $[\mathrm{A}^-]/[\mathrm{HA}]$ is between 0.1 and 10. For weak base buffers, use $\mathrm{p}K_a$ of the conjugate acid. |
| 113 🔥 | "buffer capacity" | Maximum when $[\mathrm{A}^-] = [\mathrm{HA}]$ ($\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a$). | |
| 114 ⚡ | "equivalence point" | Moles acid = moles base added. | $\mathrm{pH} \neq 7$ for weak acid/strong base titrations. |
| 115 🔥 | "half-equivalence point" | $\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a$ (for weak acid titration). | Half of HA converted to A⁻. |
| 116 🔥 | "titration curve" | Plot pH vs volume titrant added. | Shape depends on acid/base strength. |
| 117 | "indicator" | Weak acid with different color for HA vs A⁻. | Choose indicator with $\mathrm{p}K_a \approx$ equivalence point pH. |

### 8.4 Polyprotic Acids & Salts

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 118 | "polyprotic acid" | Multiple $K_a$ values: $K_{a1} \gg K_{a2} \gg K_{a3}$. | Each successive H⁺ is harder to remove. |
| 119 🔥 | "salt hydrolysis" | Conjugate of weak acid → basic. Conjugate of weak base → acidic. | Salt of strong acid + strong base → neutral (pH 7). |
| 120 | "amphoteric" | Can act as acid OR base. | e.g., H₂O, HCO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻. |

## 9. Electrochemistry — "Electrons in Motion"

> **Core reflex:** "Redox," "cell potential," "electrolysis" → half-reactions → $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ → Nernst.

### 9.1 Redox Fundamentals

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 121 ⚡ | "oxidation" | Loss of electrons. Oxidation number INCREASES. | OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain. |
| 122 ⚡ | "reduction" | Gain of electrons. Oxidation number DECREASES. | |
| 123 ⚡ | "oxidation number rules" | Element = 0. Monatomic ion = charge. F = −1 **always**. O = −2 (**except** peroxides: −1; OF₂: +2). H = +1 with nonmetals (**except** metal hydrides: −1). Sum of all oxidation numbers = overall charge. | Assign in this priority order: F → O → H → others. The most electronegative element gets its typical oxidation number first. |
| 124 | "balancing redox" (acidic) | Half-reaction method: balance atoms, add H₂O for O, H⁺ for H, e⁻ for charge. | For basic: add OH⁻ to both sides to neutralize H⁺. |

### 9.2 Cell Potentials

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 125 ⚡ | "standard cell potential $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$" | $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}$ | Cathode = reduction. Anode = oxidation. |
| 126 ⚡ | "spontaneous redox" | $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} > 0$ | Positive cell potential = spontaneous (galvanic cell). |
| 127 🔥 | "$\Delta G^\circ$ from $E^\circ$" | $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ$ | $n$ = moles of e⁻ transferred. $F = 96{,}485\;\mathrm{C/mol\;e^-}$. |
| 128 🔥 | "Nernst equation" | $E = E^\circ - \frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q$ | At $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ (298 K): $E = E^\circ - \frac{0.0592}{n}\log_{10} Q$. The constant $0.0592 = (RT\ln 10)/F$ at 298 K. ⚠️ Use $\log_{10}$, not $\ln$, with the $0.0592$ form. $n$ = moles of electrons transferred in the balanced redox reaction. |
| 129 🧠 | "Faraday's constant $F$" | $F = 96{,}485\;\mathrm{C/mol\;e^-}$ | Charge of one mole of electrons. |

### 9.3 Electrolysis

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 130 ⚡ | "electrolysis" | Non-spontaneous redox driven by external voltage. | $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} < 0$; external $V > |E^\circ_{\text{cell}}|$ needed. |
| 131 🔥 | "Faraday's Law of Electrolysis" | $m = \frac{I \times t \times M}{n \times F}$ | $I$ = current (A), $t$ = time (s), $M$ = molar mass, $n$ = e⁻ per ion. |
| 132 | "charge from current and time" | $Q = I \times t$ (coulombs) | $Q/F$ = moles of e⁻. |

## 10. Solutions & Colligative Properties

### 10.1 Concentration Units

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |

| 133 ⚡ | "molarity $M$" | $M = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{V_{\text{solution (L)}}}$ | Denominator is volume of the **final solution** (solute + solvent together), NOT volume of solvent alone. $M$ changes slightly with temperature because solution volume expands/contracts. ### 10.2 Colligative Properties

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 139 ⚡ | "colligative properties" | Depend on NUMBER of solute particles, not identity. | Four: vapor pressure lowering, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, osmotic pressure. |
| 140 🔥 | "Raoult's Law" | $P_A = X_A P_A^\circ$ | Vapor pressure of solvent above solution. For nonvolatile solute: $\Delta P = X_{\text{solute}}P^\circ$. |
| 141 🔥 | "boiling point elevation" | $\Delta T_b = i K_b m$ | $i$ = van't Hoff factor (# particles). $K_b$ = ebullioscopic constant. |
| 142 🔥 | "freezing point depression" | $\Delta T_f = i K_f m$ | $K_f$ = cryoscopic constant. |
| 143 🔥 | "osmotic pressure $\Pi$" | $\Pi = iMRT$ | $M$ = molarity. $R = 0.08206$. |
| 144 | "van't Hoff factor $i$" | $i$ = number of ions one formula unit dissociates into. | Ideal (theoretical) values: NaCl → $i = 2$, CaCl₂ → $i = 3$, FeCl₃ → $i = 4$. For covalent/molecular solutes (sugar, ethanol): $i = 1$ (no dissociation). Real solutions have $i$ slightly less than ideal due to ion pairing. |

## 11. Nuclear Chemistry — "The Nucleus"

> **Core reflex:** "Half-life," "decay," "binding energy" → $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$, $E = \Delta m c^2$.

| # | Trigger Phrase | Instant Equation | Exam Note |
|---|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| 145 ⚡ | "radioactive decay" | $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ | $\lambda$ = decay constant. |
| 146 ⚡ | "half-life $t_{1/2}$" | $t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda} = \frac{0.693}{\lambda}$ | After $n$ half-lives: $N = N_0/2^n$. |
| 147 | "activity $A$" | $A = \lambda N$ | Units: becquerel (Bq) = 1 decay/s. Curie (Ci) = $3.7\times10^{10}$ Bq. |
| 148 | "alpha decay" | ${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \to {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}\mathrm{Y} + {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}$ | Mass number −4, atomic number −2. |
| 149 | "beta decay ($\beta^-$)" | ${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \to {}^{A}_{Z+1}\mathrm{Y} + {}^{0}_{-1}\mathrm{e} + \bar{\nu}$ | Neutron → proton + electron. Atomic number +1. |
| 150 | "positron emission ($\beta^+$)" | ${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \to {}^{A}_{Z-1}\mathrm{Y} + {}^{0}_{+1}\mathrm{e} + \nu$ | Proton → neutron + positron. Atomic number −1. |
| 151 | "electron capture" | ${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} + {}^{0}_{-1}\mathrm{e} \to {}^{A}_{Z-1}\mathrm{Y} + \nu$ | Inner-shell electron captured by nucleus. |
| 152 | "gamma emission" | Excited nucleus → ground state + $\gamma$ photon. | No change in $A$ or $Z$. |
| 153 🔥 | "nuclear binding energy" | $E_B = \Delta m c^2$ | $\Delta m$ = mass defect = (mass nucleons) − (mass nucleus). |
| 154 🧠 | "mass defect" | Mass of nucleus < sum of masses of protons and neutrons. | "Missing mass" converted to binding energy. |
| 155 🔥 | "binding energy per nucleon" | $E_B/A$ | Maximum at Fe-56. Determines nuclear stability. |
