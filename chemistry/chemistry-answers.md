## C1–C8: Stoichiometry — Mole Bridge

**C1.** How many moles in $88.0\;\mathrm{g}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "moles", "88.0 g" | 1 | $n = m/M$ |

**Execute:** $n = 88.0/44.0 = 2.00\;\mathrm{mol}$.
**Reflect:** $\mathrm{g} \div \mathrm{g/mol} = \mathrm{mol}$. Sig figs: 3 (88.0) → 3 (2.00). ✓ **Ans: $2.00\;\mathrm{mol}$.**

---

**C2.** Mass of $2.50\;\mathrm{mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O}$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "mass from moles" | 1 | $m = n \times M$ |

**Execute:** $m = 2.50 \times 18.0 = 45.0\;\mathrm{g}$.
**Reflect:** $\mathrm{mol} \times \mathrm{g/mol} = \mathrm{g}$. ✓ **Ans: $45.0\;\mathrm{g}$.**

**C3.** Molecules in $0.500\;\mathrm{mol}$ of $\mathrm{N_2}$?

**Reflect:** Moles cancel dimensionally (Avogadro's number bridges mol → particles). ✓ **Ans: $3.01\times10^{23}$ molecules.**

## C9–C14: Gases

**C9.** Pressure of $2.00\;\mathrm{mol}$ ideal gas in $10.0\;\mathrm{L}$ at $300\;\mathrm{K}$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "ideal gas" | 19 | $PV = nRT$ |
| "$R$" | 20 | $R = 0.08206\;\mathrm{L\;atm\;mol^{-1}\;K^{-1}}$ |

**Execute:** $P = nRT/V = (2.00 \times 0.08206 \times 300)/10.0 = 4.92\;\mathrm{atm}$.
**Reflect:** $R$ chosen to match atm·L units. $\mathrm{mol \cdot L\;atm\;mol^{-1}\;K^{-1} \cdot K \div L = atm}$. ✓ **Ans: $4.92\;\mathrm{atm}$.**

---

**C10.** Sealed $5.00\;\mathrm{L}$ gas at $1.20\;\mathrm{atm}$, $300\;\mathrm{K}$ → heated to $400\;\mathrm{K}$. New $P$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "sealed container" (fixed $n$, $V$) | 21 | $\dfrac{P_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2}{T_2}$ |

**Execute:** $P_2 = P_1 \times T_2/T_1 = 1.20 \times 400/300 = 1.60\;\mathrm{atm}$.
**Reflect:** $V$ and $n$ constant → Gay-Lussac's Law (Card 24). $T$ must be in Kelvin (Card 197). ✓ **Ans: $1.60\;\mathrm{atm}$.**

---

**C11.** Balloon: $2.00\;\mathrm{atm}$, $300\;\mathrm{K}$, $3.00\;\mathrm{L}$ → $1.50\;\mathrm{atm}$, $280\;\mathrm{K}$. New $V$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "$n$ constant" (balloon sealed) | 21 | $\dfrac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$ |

**Execute:** $V_2 = \dfrac{P_1V_1T_2}{P_2T_1} = \dfrac{2.00 \times 3.00 \times 280}{1.50 \times 300} = 3.73\;\mathrm{L}$.
**Reflect:** Both $P$↓ and $T$↓; $V$ increase from $P$ drop outcompetes $T$ drop. ✓ **Ans: $3.73\;\mathrm{L}$.**

---

**C12.** Constant $T$: pressure doubled. What happens to volume?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "constant temperature", "isothermal" | 22 | $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ |

**Execute:** $P_2 = 2P_1 \implies V_2 = V_1/2$. Volume halves.
**Reflect:** Boyle's Law: $P \propto 1/V$. Inverse relationship. ✓ **Ans: Volume halves.**

---

**C13.** Constant $P$: gas cooled from $400\;\mathrm{K}$ to $200\;\mathrm{K}$, $V_1 = 8.00\;\mathrm{L}$. $V_2$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "constant pressure", "isobaric" | 23 | $\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$ |

**Execute:** $V_2 = 8.00 \times 200/400 = 4.00\;\mathrm{L}$.
**Reflect:** $V \propto T$ (Charles's Law). $T$ halved → $V$ halved. ✓ **Ans: $4.00\;\mathrm{L}$.**

---

**C14.** Equal volumes of $\mathrm{H_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2}$ at same $T$, $P$. Cards?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "equal volumes", "$T$ and $P$ constant" | 25 | $\dfrac{V_1}{n_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{n_2}$, so $n_1 = n_2$ |

**Execute:** Avogadro's Law: equal $V$ at same $T$ and $P$ → equal $n$.
**Reflect:** This is why mole ratios from balanced equations can be used as volume ratios for gases. ✓ **Ans: Card 25 (Avogadro's Law).**

## C15–C20: Thermochemistry Basics

**C15.** Heat to raise $50.0\;\mathrm{g}$ water from $20.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $80.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "heat" + $\Delta T$ | 33 | $q = mc\Delta T$ |
| "specific heat of water" | 34 | $c_w = 4.184\;\mathrm{J\;g^{-1}\;K^{-1}}$ |

**Execute:** $\Delta T = 80.0 - 20.0 = 60.0\;\mathrm{K}$. $q = 50.0 \times 4.184 \times 60.0 = 1.26\times10^4\;\mathrm{J} = 12.6\;\mathrm{kJ}$.
**Reflect:** $\mathrm{g \cdot J\;g^{-1}\;K^{-1} \cdot K = J}$. ΔT in °C = ΔT in K. ✓ **Ans: $12.6\;\mathrm{kJ}$.**

**Reflect:** Exothermic = system loses energy, surroundings gain it. ✓ **Ans: Exothermic, $\Delta H < 0$.**

**C17.** $100.0\;\mathrm{g}$ Al ($c = 0.900$) cools from $150.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $25.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$. $q$?

**C18.** Coffee-cup: $50.0\;\mathrm{g}$ water at $60.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ + $50.0\;\mathrm{g}$ at $20.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$. $T_f$?

**C24.** Which is larger: Na or $\mathrm{Na^+}$? Why?

## C39–C44: Equilibrium Basics

**C39.** $K_c$ for $2\mathrm{SO_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3(g)}$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |

**Execute:** Products over reactants, each raised to its coefficient.
**Reflect:** All species are gases — all appear in $K_c$. ✓ **Ans: $K_c = \dfrac{[\mathrm{SO_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{SO_2}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]}$.**

**C40.** For $\mathrm{N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3}$, does $K_c = K_p$?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "$K_p$ from $K_c$" | 90 | $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$ |

**Execute:** $\Delta n = 2 - (1 + 3) = -2 \neq 0$. So $K_p \neq K_c$. $K_p = K_c(RT)^{-2}$.
**Reflect:** $K_p = K_c$ only when $\Delta n = 0$ (equal moles of gas on both sides). ✓ **Ans: No; $\Delta n = -2$, so $K_p \neq K_c$.**

**Execute:** $K_c \gg 1$ → equilibrium lies far to the right. Products dominate.

**C42.** At equilibrium, $Q = K$. Add more reactant → $Q$? Direction of shift?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
|---------------|------|------------------|
| "reaction quotient $Q$" | 91 | $Q = [\text{products}]/[\text{reactants}]$ at any moment |
| "add reactant" | 97 | Shifts away from added substance |

**Execute:** Adding reactant increases denominator → $Q < K$. System shifts right (forward) to consume the added reactant until $Q = K$ again.
**Reflect:** Le Chatelier: stress of added reactant → shift to consume it. ✓ **Ans: $Q < K$, shifts right (forward).**

**C43.** Exothermic $2\mathrm{SO_2 + O_2 \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3}$. ↑$T$ → shift?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |

| "exothermic" | 40 | $\Delta H < 0$ |
| "increase temperature" | 99 | ↑$T$ shifts away from heat |

**Execute:** Exothermic: heat is a product. ↑$T$ adds "heat" → system shifts left (reverse, endothermic direction) to absorb the added heat.
**Reflect:** Only temperature changes $K$. For exothermic reaction, $K$ decreases as $T$ increases. ✓ **Ans: Shifts left (toward reactants).**

**C44.** $\mathrm{N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3}$. ↑$P$ → shift?

| Trigger Phrase | Card | Instant Equation |
